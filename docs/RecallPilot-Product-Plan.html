01 · Executive summary
01 Executive summary

The wedge is a number, not a feature list.

RecallPilot is an AI recall and revenue-recovery agent for dental practices in Europe. The product does not sell “AI for dentists.” It sells a monthly outcome: “We recovered 27 appointments and €8,430 of production you would otherwise have lost.”

Build the smallest closed loop that produces that number: CSV import → free recall audit → email pilot → booking → recovered-revenue report.
€199–599
Monthly plans, priced against recovered production rather than software seats.
10–12 wks
Pilot-ready MVP with one full-time developer and disciplined scope.
10 pilots
Validation target before investing in deep PMS integrations, voice or WhatsApp.

Why this can win

Outcome, not tooling

A report the practice owner forwards.

Every feature exists to produce a credible recovered-revenue number. The audit is the lead magnet; the monthly report is the retention mechanism.

Narrow first release

Email-first, CSV-first, human-approved.

No deep PMS write-back, no WhatsApp, no voice in v0.1. The first release proves demand and lift with the lowest technical and compliance risk.

Trust by design

Holdout group from day one.

Attribution is the product. A control group prevents the company from overclaiming and gives the practice a reason to believe the report.

Safety as a sales asset

Clinical questions always escalate.

RecallPilot never diagnoses, promises insurance coverage or gives clinical advice. That boundary is enforced in the agent and explained to every practice.

02 · Business context
02 Business context

A three-layer product with a hard boundary.

RecallPilot connects to the practice-management system, decides who to contact and why, and reports the appointments and production recovered. Each layer is necessary, but the outcome layer is the wedge.

Layer 1

Data

Read/write connection to the PMS: patients, appointments, recall intervals, treatment plans, contact consent. Writes back bookings and conversation outcomes.

CSV firstOpen Dental later

Layer 2

Agent

A rules engine plus LLM decides who to contact, when, on which channel, in which language, with which message. Handles replies, objections and escalation.

Rules engineHuman approval

Layer 3

Outcome

A dashboard and monthly report attributing booked appointments and recovered production to the agent, with a control group so the practice can trust the number.

HoldoutMonthly report

Product boundary

RecallPilot is not a clinical decision-support tool and not a full practice-management system. It must never diagnose, promise insurance coverage or give clinical advice. Every ambiguous clinical message is escalated to the practice. This boundary is both a safety requirement and a sales asset.

Pilot guardrails

Cohort

100–200 low-risk patients

Exclude vulnerable patients, open complaints and anyone with ambiguous consent.

Measurement

90/10 holdout

A control group from the first send so lift is measured, not assumed.

Control

Human approval

Every message is reviewed during the pilot. Auto-send is earned later.

Cadence

Weekly review

Response, bookings, front-desk load and every objection are reviewed weekly.

02 · Recall journey
02 Recall journey

From overdue list to booked appointment.

The customer journey

Step Action Detail
1 Connect the PMS OAuth/API if available; otherwise secure CSV upload or read-only export. Validate patient counts, appointment history, recall intervals and consent fields.
2 Run the free recall audit Within 24 hours: how many patients are overdue, estimated annual production at risk, and sample messages in the practice’s language.
3 Configure the agent Recall rules, languages, channels, quiet hours, providers and escalation contacts. Approve scripts and the “do not say” list.
4 Pilot for 30 days Start with 100–200 low-risk patients. Measure response, bookings and front-desk time. The practice can pause the agent at any time.
5 Review the outcome Weekly check-in and monthly recovered-revenue report. If the agreed threshold is not met, the practice does not continue or pays a reduced fee.
6 Expand Add treatment follow-up, more locations, voice and review requests. Move to annual billing with a discount.

What the AI automates vs. what requires a human

AI automates

  • Segmentation of overdue patients and recall rules.
  • Message personalization, translation and send-time optimization.
  • Reply classification, objection handling and booking into open slots.
  • Rescheduling, confirmation and no-show follow-up.
  • Reporting and attribution of recovered appointments.
  • Opt-out handling and suppression lists.

Human intervention required

  • Clinical questions (“Is this bleeding normal?”) and any medical advice.
  • Insurance/benefit exceptions and payment disputes.
  • Complaints, vulnerable patients and ambiguous requests.
  • Final approval of scripts, escalation policies and consent rules.
  • Manual audit of a sample of conversations for quality.
  • Legal/DPO review of the data-processing agreement and DPIA.

Market sequencing

Start with Germany and the Netherlands, the two markets named in the validation plan. Ship the product UI and report in English first, with the data model, templates and locale columns ready for German and Dutch. Add local-language QA before sending in those markets.

03 · Pricing and business model
03 Pricing and business model

Price against recovered production.

A recovered hygiene appointment is worth €100–250 immediately and €300–800 per year in recurring production. At €349/month, the practice needs roughly 2–4 recovered appointments to break even — a very easy sales conversation.

Tier Monthly Features and limits Target customer AI/API cost Gross margin
Starter €199 1 location; 500 patients contacted/month; SMS + email; 1 language; recall dashboard; monthly report; email support. Solo practice or first-time user; 1,000–2,500 active patients. €25–45 77–87%
Growth €349 2,000 patients/month; WhatsApp; 2 languages; treatment follow-up; voice add-on (€99 for 300 min); priority support. 1–3 location practice with an active recall problem. €60–110 68–83%
Scale €599 3 locations; 6,000 patients/month; unlimited languages; 300 voice minutes included; API; dedicated onboarding; quarterly business review. Multi-location group or dental service organisation. €120–220 63–80%

Cost assumptions: email near-zero; SMS €0.04–0.08 per message; WhatsApp €0.03–0.08 per conversation; LLM €0.01–0.05 per conversation; voice €0.10–0.25 per minute; infrastructure €5–30 per account. An SMS-heavy mix can reduce gross margin by 10–20 points, so plans must include message credits or overage pricing.

Pricing logic

Price against the value of a recovered appointment, not against other software. Use a 30-day pilot with a clear success threshold. After attribution is trusted, introduce a performance fee: €12 per recovered appointment after the first 10, capped at €299/month.

The variable to watch

Protect the first 90 days.

LTV remains attractive at 5% churn, but the first 90 days are fragile. The product must deliver visible recovered revenue quickly, or the practice will read the tool as another inbox and cancel.

Keep the team lean, raise ARPU through treatment follow-up and multi-location plans, or reach 200+ customers before hiring aggressively.

Margin guardrails

Email is near-zero; SMS costs are controlled with credits; WhatsApp template fees are reviewed monthly; voice is an add-on, not a default. Re-price if the channel mix shifts toward SMS or voice.

03 · Churn risks
03 Churn risks

Retention is the growth strategy.

A low-ACV SaaS is not profitable at 100 customers with a full team. Retention depends on visible recovered revenue within the first 90 days and a front desk that does not feel replaced.

Churn risks and mitigations

Risk Why it happens Mitigation
Practice does not see recovered revenue Attribution is weak or patient response rate is low. Control group, booking-source tracking, weekly reports, 30-day pilot with a clear success threshold.
Front desk rejects the tool Fear of losing control or extra work. Human-in-the-loop inbox, staff training, “assistant not replacement” positioning, quick wins in week one.
PMS integration breaks API changes, export errors, vendor restrictions. Monitoring, CSV fallback, integration status page, partner agreements, error alerts.
Patient complaints or opt-outs Too many messages, wrong tone, language errors. Frequency caps, quiet hours, easy opt-out, human review of scripts, local-language QA.
Practice closure or merger Market consolidation. Multi-location plans, diversify across countries, expand to veterinary/allied health.
PMS vendor bundles recall Incumbent adds AI features. Stay ahead on outcome reporting, multilingual depth and integrations; partner rather than compete where possible.

Retention playbook

Week one

Deliver a visible win.

Import, audit and first approved campaign within the first week. Show the practice one booked appointment it can trace.

Every week

Send the scorecard.

Response, bookings, recovered production and escalations. Make the number impossible to miss.

Every month

Review the holdout.

Compare treated and control groups, explain the assumptions and agree on one expansion.

03 · Validation plan
03 Validation plan

Validate before writing production code.

The product plan assumes the business case is validated in parallel with the first build. The audit is both the lead magnet and the validation instrument.

Week Action Success criterion
1 30 interviews with practice managers in Germany and the Netherlands; ask for current recall process, overdue patient count, staff hours, PMS and willingness to pay. At least 20 interviews completed; 10 practices share a real overdue-patient number.
2 Build a free “Recall Revenue Audit” using CSV upload. Run it for 5 clinics. Deliver a one-page report with estimated revenue at risk. 5 audits delivered; at least 3 clinics say the number is credible.
3 Pre-sell 10 pilots at €199/month for 90 days, 50% upfront. Use a concierge workflow: manual segmentation, AI-assisted messages, human review. 10 paying pilots, or 5 pilots plus 5 strong written commitments.
4 Run the first pilot messages, measure response and bookings, interview the front desk and document every objection. 20%+ response rate; 10+ booked appointments in the first month; front-desk time under 2 hours/week.

Kill criteria

Do not build the full product if fewer than 3 clinics will pay after 30 interviews; the response rate is below 10%; fewer than 5 appointments are recovered per clinic per month; or the PMS integration cannot be solved with CSV or one API within 6 weeks. In that case, pivot to veterinary clinics with the same engine or to SwitchboardAI.

04 · Recall workflows
04 Recall workflows

The recall loop, step by step.

Every step produces a record the practice can inspect. Nothing is sent without an eligibility check, and every booking can be traced back to a campaign and a message.

Core workflows

1

Audit

Upload CSV without full account → normalize and validate → overdue cohorts and at-risk production → sample messages → shareable report → convert to org.

2

Campaign

Rules engine creates tasks → campaign with 90/10 holdout → eligibility checks → LLM drafts within approved templates → human approval → email send → inbound reply → classification/escalation → booking.

3

Booking

Patient picks a slot or requests a time → booking record plus attribution token → confirmation/ICS → front desk confirms. PMS write-back comes later.

4

Attribution

Compare treatment vs holdout booking rates. Report gross attributed revenue and control-adjusted lift separately. Label small samples as directional.

Attribution formula

lift = treated booking rate − control booking rate. Incremental revenue = lift × treated patients × average production. Report gross attributed revenue separately and never present it as causal without the control group.

Before a message is sent

Check Rule
ConsentChannel consent must be granted for the selected channel. Unknown or expired consent blocks the send.
Opt-outAny prior opt-out for the patient or channel suppresses all future messages, regardless of campaign.
Quiet hoursSend only inside clinic-defined windows in the clinic timezone, including local holidays.
Frequency capMaximum messages per patient per 30 days; no duplicate outreach across active campaigns.
Recent appointmentSkip patients already booked or seen since the task was generated.
Open conversationDo not start a new thread while a human is replying or an escalation is unresolved.
04 · Architecture
04 Technical architecture

One TypeScript codebase, few moving parts.

The brief proposes Next.js + NestJS + FastAPI + Redis + n8n. For a small team, simplify to a modular Next.js app, a Postgres-backed worker and shared domain packages. Add Python only when voice or heavier NLP requires it.

Why not the full stack from the brief?

NestJS, FastAPI, Redis and n8n add deployment and operational overhead before the product has proven demand. Next.js route handlers plus a pg-boss worker are enough for the first thousand patients. Revisit the split when voice, heavy document processing or throughput demand it.

04 · Data and compliance
04 Data and compliance

Tenant isolation is the foundation.

Patient data is sensitive. The schema, RLS policies, retention jobs and audit trail are product requirements, not infrastructure details.

Product repository layout

RecallPilot-App/ ├─ apps/ │ ├─ web/ # Next.js 15: dashboard, inbox, booking, audit, APIs │ └─ worker/ # pg-boss: imports, sends, classification, reports ├─ packages/ │ ├─ db/ # Drizzle schema, migrations, seeds, RLS tests │ ├─ core/ # rules, segments, attribution, plans, i18n, zod │ ├─ integrations/ # PMS and channel adapter interfaces │ └─ ui/ # shared shadcn components and brand tokens ├─ supabase/ # local config and policies ├─ .github/workflows/ # CI └─ package.json # npm workspaces

Minimum data model

  • Tenancy: organizations, locations, users, memberships, providers
  • Patients: patients, patient_channel_consents, appointments
  • Recall: recall_rules, recall_tasks
  • Outreach: campaigns, campaign_members, conversations, messages, message_events
  • Outcomes: bookings, attribution_results, report_runs
  • Operations: imports, import_rows, integration_connections, opt_outs, usage_events, subscriptions, plan_catalog, audit_logs

Attribution mechanics

  • Randomize campaign members 90/10 into treatment/holdout.
  • Stratify by location, treatment type and overdue bucket.
  • Outcome window: 30 days initially, later 60/90.
  • Revenue from PMS production value, or clinic-configured average.
  • Booking tokens prevent double counting and tie revenue to the campaign.
  • Show confidence and mark small samples as directional.

Security and compliance

  • Supabase EU region; RLS on every tenant table; cross-tenant tests in CI.
  • Integration credentials in Supabase Vault; service keys only on server/worker.
  • Consent enforced per channel; unknown consent blocks sending; global opt-out suppression.
  • Quiet hours in clinic timezone; frequency caps; SPF/DKIM/DMARC; List-Unsubscribe.
  • Clinical, insurance, complaint and ambiguous replies always escalate to a human.
  • Append-only audit log; PII redaction in Sentry/logs; retention and deletion jobs.
  • DPA/DPIA templates, subprocessor list and incident runbook before scale.
  • Raw audit-lead uploads purged on a short retention schedule.

05 · R1 acceptance
05 R1 acceptance

Done means a clinic can trust the number.

R1 is complete when a practice can upload its own data, see an audit it believes, and share it with a colleague without manual help from the team.

R1 acceptance criteria

  • A 5,000-row clinic CSV imports and produces an audit in under 60 seconds.
  • Mapping and validation errors are visible and downloadable; no silent data loss.
  • RLS tests prove one org cannot access another org’s patients or reports.
  • Report token is unguessable, revocable and expires.
  • The report is readable on mobile and labels every estimate as an assumption.
  • A new user can go from signup to an imported audit without manual intervention.
  • Synthetic seed covers 3 locations, ~2,500 patients, messy rows and two CSV variants.
  • Raw audit-lead uploads are purged on the defined retention schedule.

Synthetic data for R1

Until a real clinic export is available, seed the product with a realistic synthetic clinic: 3 locations, ~2,500 patients, hygiene and treatment recall gaps, consent flags, duplicates and deliberately malformed rows. Export it in two variants — comma/ISO dates and semicolon/DD.MM.YYYY — so the importer is tested against European formats while the UI stays English.

Voice and tone

Clear

Say the number.

Lead with recovered appointments and production. Avoid vague claims like “streamline” or “leverage AI.”

Calm

Never pressure the patient.

Messages are helpful, short and easy to ignore. Opt-out is always one click or one word away.

Careful

Escalate, don’t guess.

Clinical, insurance and complaint language is never improvised. The agent hands off to a human.

Writing rules

Use sentence case. Prefer short sentences and concrete nouns. Show the assumption behind every estimate. Never imply clinical advice, guaranteed coverage or guaranteed revenue. Localize with native review before sending in German or Dutch.

Copy examples

Instead of Say
“Leverage AI to streamline your recall.”“Recover overdue appointments from your own patient list.”
“Guaranteed revenue growth.”“Estimated recovered production, with the assumptions shown.”
“Your patients are overdue.”“Three simple ways to get back on track.”
“Book now!!!”“Choose a time that works for you.”
